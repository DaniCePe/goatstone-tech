The three methods (NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2)

Clear applies logical techniques to sanitize data in all user-addressable storage locations, protecting against simple non-invasive recovery. Used when a device stays with the same owner or inside the same organization.

Purge applies physical or logical techniques that make recovery infeasible even with state-of-the-art laboratory methods, while keeping the device usable. Firmware commands (ATA Sanitize, NVMe Sanitize, cryptographic erase where its conditions are met) are the standard route for drives that will be resold.

Destroy makes recovery infeasible and the media unusable: shredding, crushing, or incineration by our downstream partners. For flash media, destruction means damaging the flash chips; degaussing does nothing to an SSD and is not a Destroy method under Rev. 2.

Verification and validation

Every job is verified (the tool result, error status, and drive health are checked) and then validated (a named person reviews the result and accepts or rejects it). A rejected job is re-run with a different technique or escalated to a stronger method. A finished progress bar alone never counts as proof.

Your certificate

Each destroyed or purged drive gets a certificate carrying the fields NIST Rev. 2 Sec. 4.6 calls for: manufacturer, model, serial number, media type and source, method and technique, tool and version, verification method, and the validator's name, title, date, location, and signature.

About "DoD wipes"

Vendors still advertise multi-pass "DoD 5220.22-M" wipes. That standard's overwrite specifications were removed by the DoD itself in 2006, and multi-pass overwriting is the wrong tool for modern drives, especially SSDs with overprovisioned space an overwrite cannot reach. The current federal guidance is NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2, and that is what we follow.

Questions

Overwrite or firmware erase: which do my drives get?
SSDs and NVMe drives get firmware Purge (Sanitize or cryptographic erase), never multi-pass overwrite. Spinning drives going to resale get firmware Purge; drives staying with the same owner may get Clear.

What if a drive fails or is locked?
It escalates: a different technique, then Destroy, and the certificate records what actually happened.
